Capture specification: fence-rail photographs (workcells 030 and 090)

Correction (2026-10-14, Phase 9 research, 09-RESEARCH.md section A). The "35-60 deg apart" instruction below is one pair's property, not a rule, and the 60 deg ceiling is unsupported. The engine's error model (lower_edge.two_view) shows the decisive photograph is a LOW, near-level one: phone at max(0.30 m, rail height + 0.12 m) above the floor, level (about 4 deg down), standoff about (rail length + 0.4) / 1.04 m clamped to 2.0-3.5 m, both posts and the floor strip under the rail in frame. Paired with any existing higher view this predicts about 0.7 cm on the 090 right rail; a HIGH tilted photo alone stays at 3.4 cm and is needed only when no partner view exists. The capture check of Phase 9 runs the engine's formula on each pair and names what limits it; nothing below is a gate. The shot list is rewritten in plan 09-01.

For the person holding the phone. Derived 2026-10-13 from the code and the recorded runs: a capture procedure, not a validated field method; distances come from recorded cameras, not from a site survey. 1. Goal

A usable capture lets the pipeline publish the gap between a fence rail's lower edge and the floor: LE lifts the edge to 3D from two photos and publishes only at sigma <= 2.5 cm (trusted at <= 1 cm, LE:66). The existing sets cannot. Of the three rails (030 right_fence, 090 left_fence and right_fence) the 030 fence panel appears in photo 1 only and the 090 left rail was drawn in photo 2 only (one view cannot be lifted). The one 090 rail with two views (right, photos 1 and 3) was shot from almost the same elevation (lines of sight 22.5 and 23.5 deg below horizontal, 3.5 m away): dihedral 9.08 deg, and even with hand-drawn rail masks sigma is 3.59 cm (18.72 cm, not published). The rails are 84-96 px thick and only 330-410 px long (11-14 % of the 2880 px width), and automatic segmentation returns the whole panel, not the rail (P5/05-DIAG-FLOOR-GAP-2026-10-12.md). The new photos must give, per rail, two views that differ in height or distance, a thick straight rail with the floor strip under it, the e-stop for the metric scale, and every declared object twice.

3. Whole-cell shots still needed (S1, S3)

At least two wide photos per cell from different positions: every declared object fully visible in at least two photos (rail photos count) and the floor visible. 030 (8 objects): robot, cart, guard, left and right light curtain, left and right post, right fence. 090 (9): the same plus the left fence (cells/030.json:3, cells/090.json:3). No people in frame (the sets are private, MIR run manifests).

Why: S3 confirms an identity only with >= 2 views (associate.py:186) and stops when any declared object is unbound (associate.py:173-176); S1 needs a floor in some photo (segment.py:127) and finds classes by text (sam3.py:32-40: fence, robot, cart, light curtain, bollard, guard); S2a matches every photo pair (geometry_mvs.py:28), so photos must overlap.

5. What the engineer declares afterwards, and what runs next

  • Shot log (read by no stage): per photo, number, rail or objects in view, LOW/MID/HIGH, e-stop yes/no, name and date. Left/right is read in photo 01 (the entrance overview), as the existing labels "left/right of the entrance (camera 1)" do (RR 030 layer).
  • Identity declaration: which instance is left_fence / right_fence (and left/right post, curtain) in each photo. A real S1 binds an object only when it is the one of its class (associate.py:122); same-class pairs stay ambiguous and S3 stops (associate.py:173-176). Lead ruling G3: an engineer declaration with value, who, when and source, never the frozen masks (P5/05-CONTEXT.md:114; declared/1: .planning/phases/06-compliance-with-action/06-04-SUMMARY.md:11, argus/pipeline/verdict.py:65-72). Runnable since 07-03: after S1, S3 writes a contact sheet per photo (evidence/instances/<frame>.jpg, every instance outlined with its key); declare each same-class object with panoptes declare-identity --cell <cell> --run <run> --object <id> --instance <frame/class/index> --who <name> --source <what you looked at> (identity/1; never declared/1, which holds S9 inputs only, argus/verdict/html.py:23).
  • E-stop declaration: after the first S2c on the new set, read each photo that shows the e-stop on evidence/estop/<frame>.jpg (a grid in the photo's own pixels) or on the photo itself, and declare its axis column and the rows of the red head's top and the grey base's bottom with panoptes declare-estop --cell <cell> --run <run> --frame <frame> --axis-x <x> --head-top-row <y0> --base-bottom-row <y1> --who <name> --source <what you looked at> (estop/1; two or more photos are triangulated, a single photo needs --box <x0> <x1> <y0> <y1> around the e-stop); S2c then fits the e-stop and the 4 % gate decides whether the set gets a metric scale.
  • Then, each paid step only on your go-ahead with its cost stated first (P7 ruling 3; the guard is the owner's written approval, PANOPTES_PAID_BUDGET_USD is not enforced by panoptes run): S1 (SAM 3) -> S2a (geometry, Modal) -> S2b -> S3 with your declaration -> S3b -> component job -> S9 (argus/pipeline/cli.py:243-308; S3 reads the S2b geometry, so it cannot precede S2a); the run then continues through S2c (after your e-stop declaration), S2d, S4a (paid, A100), S4b to S8 and S9; the estimate per set is in HANDOFF.md ("Paid steps on a new set"). S2a does N(N-1)/2 RoMa pairs for N photos (geometry_mvs.py:28; 3 photos = 3 pairs in MIR checks/clean-gpu/090); the frozen sets' RoMa, MoGe and dense ledgers are 0.019 + 0.009 USD on L4 (MIR checks/clean-gpu/spend-ledger*.json), without the DA3-BASE start: the estimate for the actual N comes first.
